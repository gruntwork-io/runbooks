title: <Chapter>
---

The `<Chapter>` block splits a long runbook into chapters, which Runbooks shows one at a time. Use it for a runbook that's best worked through in phases, such as setting up, deploying, and checking a service.

## Basic usage

```mdx
# Deploy the service

This runbook sets up the service, deploys it, and checks that it's up.

<Chapter title="Set up">

## Configure the service

<Inputs id="service" path="templates/service" />

</Chapter>

<Chapter title="Deploy">

## Deploy

<Command id="deploy" path="scripts/deploy.sh" inputsId="service" />

</Chapter>

<Chapter title="Verify">

## Check the service

<Check id="health" command="curl --fail https://my-service.example.com/health" />

</Chapter>
```

Leave a blank line after the opening tag and before the closing tag, so that the content between them is read as markdown.

Write each `<Chapter>` at the top level of the runbook. A `<Chapter>` inside another block or another chapter shows a configuration error, with its content where it is.

## Moving between chapters

In a runbook with two or more chapters, the chapter on screen starts with a bar that stays at the top of the runbook as you scroll it:

- **Contents** opens the table of contents, which lists every chapter by number and title, with how many of its [steps](/authoring/blocks/#progress) are done, such as "1/3". A chapter whose steps are all done gets a check mark. Pick a chapter to go to it.
- The bar names the chapter on screen, such as "Chapter 2 of 3 · Deploy".
- The **‹** and **›** buttons go to the previous and next chapters.

Each chapter ends with links to the previous and next chapters. The [command palette](/intro/ui_tour/) has **Go to chapter…**, which lists the chapters, and typing a chapter's title there offers it directly.

A runbook with no chapters, or with only one, has none of these: it shows all of its content at once, as it would without the block.

A runbook opens at its first chapter, and so does a new or [resumed](/commands/sessions/#resuming-a-session) session. A resumed session's blocks come back as the user left them, so the table of contents shows which chapters are done. Editing the runbook while [watch mode](/commands/watch/) reloads it keeps the chapter on screen.

## Content outside chapters

Content outside every chapter shows with each of them, where it is in the runbook. Put the runbook's title and introduction above the first chapter, so they head every chapter. A block outside the chapters, such as a `<Prerequisites>` above the first one, is on screen whichever chapter is.

## Blocks in chapters

Chapters only change what's on screen. A hidden chapter's blocks stay loaded, so:

- Block IDs are shared by the whole runbook: each must be unique across every chapter.
- A block can use the [inputs and outputs](/authoring/inputs-and-outputs/) of a block in any other chapter, and the credentials of an auth block in another chapter.
- A block keeps its state while another chapter is on screen: a form keeps its values, and a command that's running keeps running.
- The [progress](/authoring/blocks/#progress) bar in the title bar counts the steps of every chapter.

A link to something in another chapter, such as the link a block shows to a block it's waiting for, goes to that chapter first.

Find in page (**Cmd+F** on macOS, or **Ctrl+F** on Windows and Linux) searches the chapter on screen and the content outside the chapters, as does the command palette's **Jump to section**.

## Props

### Required props

- `title` (string): the chapter's name, shown in the chapter bar, the table of contents, and the command palette. A chapter without one shows a configuration error, and is named by its number, such as "Chapter 2".

## Instruction mode

Chapters work the same in [instruction mode](/intro/instruction-mode/). The table of contents counts each chapter's steps as the user marks them done.

## Testing

[Runbook tests](/authoring/testing/) don't see chapters. They run the blocks in document order, whichever chapter each one is in, and a step can use the outputs of a block in an earlier chapter.

The [chapters feature demo](https://github.com/gruntwork-io/runbooks/tree/main/testdata/feature-demos/chapters) splits a runbook into three chapters, with a command in one chapter that reads the output of a command in another.
:::
